> This guide covers Mythic GME 1.7.0 and later.

When you open the Mythic app for the first time, you land right on the Fate Chart. From there, you move between Lists, Scenes, and Meaning Tables as your story unfolds. Your Journals stay stored locally on your device. If you play on iPhone, iPad, or the Mac App Store version, you can turn on **Use iCloud Sync** to keep them synced in iCloud.

## What to have ready

Before you start your first session, grab a few things:

- The [Mythic Game Master Emulator 2e book](https://www.drivethrurpg.com/en/product/422929/mythic-game-master-emulator-second-edition) from Word Mill Creative for rules context and odds guidance
- Your favorite RPG system and a character sheet ready to play
- The mobile or desktop Mythic GME app installed on your device

## Install the app and name your first Journal

Download, install, and open the mobile or desktop app. It creates your first Journal automatically and names it **My Journal**.

To rename it, open **Settings**, go to **Files**, tap the menu on the Journal's row, and choose **Rename Journal**.

## Start on the Fate Chart

On first launch, the app opens to the **Fate Chart**. If you prefer **Fate Check**, you can switch your default view in **Settings** anytime.

Set your **Chaos Factor** (1 to 9) to match your starting situation, pick your Odds, and roll. The app highlights Yes, No, Exceptional Yes, and Exceptional No results right in the Adventure Log.

If you want to type a question before each roll, tap the gear icon on the Fate screen and turn on **Prompt for Question Before Roll**. The app saves every Fate roll, Scene Check, Random Event, and Meaning Table roll to the **Adventure Log**, so you only need a Scene's **Summary** for your own story notes.

## Change settings for each screen

Most screens have a gear icon that lets you adjust settings for that screen.

- **Adventure Log detail.** On a phone, open **Settings**, go to the **Game Behavior** tab, and open **Adventure Log Settings** to show more roll detail. On a tablet or desktop, tap the gear icon on the **Adventure Log** panel.
- **Date and time format.** In version 1.7.0 and later, open **Settings** and go to **Date and Time** to pick how timestamps appear. **Date format** offers **Automatic**, **Day/Month/Year**, **Month/Day/Year**, and **Year-Month-Day**. **Time format** offers **Automatic**, **24-hour**, and **12-hour**. **Automatic** follows your device's region and clock settings.

## Core tools you use every session

Adding a few Characters and Threads to your Lists before your first game gives Random Events something to work with right away. During play, you will spend most of your time across four areas:

- **Fate Chart and Fate Check.** Ask yes-or-no questions, pick odds, roll, and read the answer.
- **Lists.** Track Characters, Threads, and Features. Random Events pull from these lists and bring familiar people, goals, and hazards back into your story.
- **Scenes.** Give each scene a title and summary. Jot notes as you play, or wait until the scene wraps up and summarize what happened.
- **Meaning Tables.** Roll on Action Meaning 1, Action Meaning 2, and other tables whenever you need a pair of words to spark an idea.

## How the layout works on your device

The layout adapts to your screen size:

- **Phone.** Each area is its own screen. Switch between them from the navigation drawer in the top left. Try landscape mode when you want more room on screen while playing.
- **Tablet and desktop.** The Fate screen uses panels you can arrange, such as the Adventure Log, Fate controls, and Lists. The **Game Tables Pull-Out** on the right edge opens Meaning Tables and Event Focus, and the navigation rail opens Scenes, Dice Studio, and the rest of the screens. Use **Edit Panels** from any panel menu or title row to rearrange panels or pick a starting layout.

## A first session flow

When you are ready to play, follow this loop for your first scene:

1. Set your Chaos Factor on the Fate Chart.
2. Create a Scene with a short summary. A new Scene automatically takes your Journal's current Chaos Factor, so there is nothing extra to enter.
3. Ask your first yes-or-no Fate Question on the Fate Chart and roll.
4. If you get stuck, roll on Meaning Tables for inspiration.
5. Add Characters, Threads, and Features to your Lists as they come up in play.
6. Adjust your Chaos Factor between scenes as control shifts in the story.

Keep the Mythic GME 2e book nearby early on, and lean on **Likely** or **50/50** odds while you get comfortable. Remember that the Chaos Factor changes both how likely a Yes answer is and how often a Random Event happens.
